1. Baseline Analytic Model

Due to the massive array sizes of modern caches ($N > 10^7$ bits), memory yield estimations require mapping global architectural yields down to an astronomical cell-level reliability.

A. Failure Probability Mapping

For an array of $N$ cells to successfully operate at a target product Yield $Y$, every single cell must function correctly.

$$Y_{array} = (1 - P_{fail\_cell})^N$$

Solving for the allowed cell failure probability yields:

$$P_{fail\_cell} = 1 - Y^{1/N}$$

Note: For excessively large N, this is modeled computationally as $\frac{-\ln(Y)}{N}$ to bypass standard float-precision underflow limits.

B. Inverse Normal CDF ($Z$-Score)

SRAM bit-cell intrinsic parameters (like Vmin) are statistically mapped as Normal (Gaussian) distributions driven by Random Dopant Fluctuation ($\sigma$). To isolate the supply guardband, we locate the required $Z$-score for the extreme tail fail probability.

Our calculation engine mathematically maps this target probability using an internal JS analytic precision port of Moro's Inverse CDF Approximation.

$$V_{ccmin\_cache} = \mu + Z \cdot \sigma$$

2. Comparison to Gumbel-Based Array Vccmin (Thomas et al.)

In advanced statistical SRAM modeling literature (such as Extreme Value Theory approaches applied by Thomas et al.), evaluating array $V_{ccmin}$ does not necessarily require mapping backward to a static cell-level $Z$-score constraint. Instead, the overall chip's $V_{ccmin}$ is evaluated directly as an extreme value distribution.

Because the final Array $V_{ccmin}$ is determined by the strictly worst-case maximum failure voltage out of $N$ independent Gaussian cells, the array's $V_{ccmin}$ converges elegantly to a Type I Extreme Value Distribution (Gumbel Distribution).

Gumbel Distribution Parameters

For an underlying bit-cell $V_{min}$ defined by early Mean ($\mu$) and Sigma ($\sigma$), the Gumbel distribution for the maximum of an $N$-cell array scales with:

  • Location Parameter ($\beta_N$) $\rightarrow$ Expected mode of the maximum bounds:

    $$ \beta_N \approx \mu + \sigma \sqrt{2 \ln N} - \sigma \frac{\ln(\ln N) + \ln(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\ln N}} $$

  • Scale Parameter ($\alpha_N$) $\rightarrow$ Spread/Variance of the extreme bounds:

    $$ \alpha_N \approx \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2 \ln N}} $$

Analytic Derivation of Cache Vccmin

Under this elegant formulation, the Array Yield $Y$ simplifies rigorously to the standard Gumbel Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF):

$$ Y = \exp\left[-\exp\left(-\frac{V_{ccmin} - \beta_N}{\alpha_N}\right)\right] $$

Resulting in the precise closed-form boundary requirement limit for the chip:

$$ V_{ccmin\ (Gumbel)} = \beta_N - \alpha_N \ln(-\ln Y) $$

Conclusion: Validation of our Engine

The Gumbel model acts as a highly accurate continuous analytic approximation for subset maximums and beautifully simplifies the calculation stack forward. Conversely, our Calculator's algorithm focuses heavily on evaluating the strict per-cell probability ($\frac{-\ln Y}{N}$) backwards, using high-precision Inverse mapping.

Both methods validate each other: For production-level large macro arrays (e.g., $N > 10^7$ bits) evaluated at industrial yields ($Y \approx 99\%$), the Gumbel framework and our Calculator's fundamental Normal Inverse algorithm converge identically, securely yielding the exact same bounding voltages up to floating-point precision.
